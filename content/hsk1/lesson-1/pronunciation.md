### The Four Tones (四声)

Chinese has four basic tones. Tones distinguish meaning in Chinese.

| Tone | Pitch | Tone mark | Example | Meaning |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|--------|
| 1st tone | high level (55) | ā | 妈 mā | mother |
| 2nd tone | rising (35) | á | 麻 má | hemp |
| 3rd tone | dipping (214) | ǎ | 马 mǎ | horse |
| 4th tone | falling (51) | à | 骂 mà | to scold |

### Chinese Syllables (音节)

A Chinese syllable is generally made up of three parts: an initial, a final, and a tone. One Chinese character corresponds to one syllable. A syllable may have no initial, but must have a final and a tone.

| Syllable | Initial | Final | Tone |
|----------|---------|-------|------|
| māo 猫 (cat) | m | ao | 1st |
| yú 鱼 (fish) | — | ü | 2nd |
| jiě 姐 (older sister) | j | ie | 3rd |
| èr 二 (two) | — | er | 4th |

**Note:** When i or ü forms a syllable on its own, y is added before the final and the dots on ü are removed. When u forms a syllable on its own, w is added before the final.

### Third Tone Sandhi (三声变调)

When two third-tone syllables appear in a row, the first one changes to a second tone (3 + 3 → 2 + 3). When writing pinyin, the original tone mark is always used.

| Original | | Changed |
|----------|-|---------|
| nǐ 你 + hǎo 好 | → | ní hǎo |
| kě 可 + yǐ 以 | → | ké yǐ |
| fǔ 辅 + dǎo 导 | → | fú dǎo |
